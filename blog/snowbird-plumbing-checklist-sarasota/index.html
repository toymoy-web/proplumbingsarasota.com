The Snowbird's Plumbing Checklist Before Leaving Your Sarasota Home

A home that sits empty for months doesn't stop being pressurized just because nobody's using it anymore. A supply line, a washing machine hose, or a corroded valve can fail in July just as easily as any other month, and if it does, water can run for weeks before anyone actually notices. Here's what's genuinely worth doing before you lock the door and head north.

The Main Decision

Shut Off Everything, or Isolate Specific Lines?

Shutting off the main valve is the safest option if nothing in the home needs to keep running while you're away. But if you have an irrigation system, a pool, or a refrigerator ice maker you want to leave operating, isolating just those specific lines is usually the smarter middle ground rather than leaving the entire house pressurized for months at a stretch. Either way, the goal is the same: nothing should be under pressure that doesn't genuinely need to be.

The Water Heater

Why It Needs Attention Too

A water heater sitting at full temperature for months with zero hot water demand is one of the more wasteful and damaging things left running in a vacant Florida home. Sarasota's hard water bakes an increasingly thick mineral layer onto the bottom of the tank the entire time it cycles with no actual use. Turning it off, or at minimum lowering it to its lowest setting, before an extended absence protects the tank and avoids paying to heat water nobody's using.

Drains

Preventing Sewer Gas During a Long Absence

Every drain trap relies on standing water to block sewer gas, and over several months of no use, that water evaporates. Covering sinks and drains, or asking a neighbor or home-watch service to run water through them periodically, reduces the odds of coming home to a sewer smell along with everything else on this list.

Before You Lock Up

A Final Walkthrough Worth Doing

Check under every sink and behind the washing machine and refrigerator for any existing dampness or corrosion on a supply line. A connection that's already weakening is exactly what tends to fail during months of unattended pressure. If anything looks even slightly questionable, replacing it before you leave costs far less than the water damage from it failing while you're gone.

Appliances Left Behind

Washing Machines, Ice Makers, and Dishwashers

The braided supply lines behind a washing machine are one of the most common sources of a major water loss in a vacant home. A single failed line can run continuously for the entire time you're away with nothing to trigger a shutoff. If you're isolating specific lines rather than shutting off the whole house, the washing machine's hot and cold supply valves are worth closing individually even if you leave everything else pressurized. The same logic applies to a refrigerator's ice maker line and a dishwasher's supply connection, both are easy to overlook precisely because they're not part of the usual main-shutoff conversation most people already know to have.

Humidity Control

Why This Connects to Plumbing Too

Keeping the air conditioning running at a moderate setpoint, rather than turning it off entirely, controls indoor humidity while you're gone, and that matters for plumbing too. A humid, unconditioned house accelerates condensation on pipes and fittings in ways that can mask a slow drip as ordinary moisture, making a developing leak harder to notice on your return until it's already caused real damage.

Coming Home

What to Check Before Turning Everything Back On

Before restoring full water pressure, walk the house and look for any signs of a problem that developed while you were away, staining, warped flooring, or a musty smell in an enclosed space. Turn the main valve back on slowly rather than all at once, and check the water heater's connections before fully restoring power or gas to it. Months of inactivity is exactly when a weak connection tends to reveal itself.

A Realistic Timeline

How Long This Actually Takes

None of this checklist requires a full day. Testing and photographing the main shutoff, adjusting the water heater, isolating any lines you want left running, and doing a final walkthrough under sinks and behind appliances realistically takes under an hour for most homes. The cost, in most cases, is nothing at all beyond whatever a plumber charges to fix anything the walkthrough actually turns up. Makes it one of the highest-value pre-departure tasks on a much longer moving-out checklist.

Common Questions

Related Questions

Should I turn off my water heater completely or just lower the temperature?

Either works. Turning it off fully avoids any energy cost and protects the tank most, while lowering it to the lowest setting is a reasonable middle ground if you'd rather not deal with reheating a fully cold tank from scratch on your return in the fall months.

What if I want to keep my irrigation system running while I'm away?

Isolate just the irrigation line rather than leaving the whole house pressurized. Ask a plumber to confirm the right valve to close for your specific setup, since every home's configuration is a little different.

How often should someone check on a vacant Sarasota home over the summer?

Weekly checks are common practice among snowbirds who use a home-watch service, since a failure caught within a week causes far less damage than one that runs undetected for the months between visits.
