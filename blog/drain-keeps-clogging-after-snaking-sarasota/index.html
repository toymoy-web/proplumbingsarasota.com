Why Does My Drain Keep Clogging After I Snake It?

A drain that clogs again within weeks of being cleared isn't bad luck, it's a signal worth paying attention to. Cabling pushes through a blockage but doesn't remove what's coating the inside of the pipe, and if that's the real problem, the same clog will simply come back on roughly the same schedule.

Why Cabling Alone Isn't Enough

What a Snake Actually Does, and Doesn't Do

A standard drain snake pushes or grinds a path through a blockage, clearing the immediate clog. What it doesn't do is scour the walls of the pipe. Grease buildup, mineral scale, or fine root growth coating the interior stays in place, ready to catch the next thing that goes down the drain. If the underlying coating is thick enough, a full re-clog can happen within weeks.

Root Intrusion

Why This Is Especially Common in Established Neighborhoods

Mature, established neighborhoods with decades-old landscaping often have sewer laterals old enough for tree roots to have found every joint in the line. Cabling can punch through root intrusion temporarily, but the roots regrow, sometimes within weeks. Exactly the pattern of a repeat clog that keeps returning at roughly the same interval each time.

How to Actually Diagnose It

Camera Inspection Before Another Cable Job

If a drain has clogged more than once, running a camera through the line before scheduling another snake job is the only real way to see what's actually happening inside the pipe. A camera distinguishes between grease buildup, root intrusion, and a structural issue like a bellied or cracked section, and each of those needs a different fix.

The Actual Fix

Hydro Jetting vs. Repair

💦

Hydro Jetting

For buildup or root growth on an otherwise sound pipe, high-pressure water scours the full interior, clearing far more than a cable ever could.

🔧

Spot Repair

For a localized crack or a section with confirmed root intrusion into a joint, a targeted repair fixes the structural cause directly.

♼

Trenchless Lining

For a line with multiple problem areas along its length, a liner restores the pipe without a full trench excavation.

Kitchen vs. Main Line

Why the Location Changes What's Likely Going On

A kitchen sink that clogs repeatedly is more likely dealing with grease buildup than root intrusion. Roots typically enter through the main sewer lateral rather than an interior branch line. A main line or an exterior cleanout clogging repeatedly is the more common location for root intrusion specifically. That's the section of pipe running through soil where tree roots actually reach it. Knowing which part of the system keeps clogging is itself a useful piece of information to share when you call. It narrows down the likely cause before anyone even arrives.

DIY Snaking

Why a Store-Bought Snake Sometimes Makes It Worse

A consumer-grade drain snake can push a clog further down the line rather than actually breaking it up, sometimes compacting it into a harder blockage in a spot that's more difficult to reach afterward. It can also scratch or gouge the inside of older, more brittle pipe, creating a rough surface that catches debris more easily going forward. None of this means DIY snaking is always a bad idea. It does mean a persistent, repeat clog is a better candidate for professional cabling or jetting than another attempt with the same store-bought tool.

Preventive Jetting

Breaking the Cycle for Good

For a property with a documented history of root intrusion, scheduling hydro jetting on a proactive annual or biennial basis, rather than waiting for the next full clog, keeps the line genuinely open rather than letting root growth rebuild between reactive service calls. This costs more over time than doing nothing. Meaningfully less than the cycle of repeat emergency calls and eventual structural repair that an untreated, worsening root intrusion problem leads to.

A Quick Self-Check

Before You Call, Try This

Note exactly how long it took for the drain to clog again after the last service, days, weeks, or months. That interval itself is useful diagnostic information. A clog that returns within days points toward something actively blocking flow right where the cable reached; one that takes months suggests slower buildup or gradual root regrowth. Either detail, shared upfront, helps a plumber decide whether a camera inspection is worth doing before attempting another cable pass.

Common Questions

Related Questions

Is it normal for a drain to need snaking more than once a year?

Occasional single-fixture clogs happen to everyone, but a drain needing repeated snaking within weeks or months of the same location points to an underlying cause worth diagnosing, not routine maintenance.

Will hydro jetting damage my pipes?

Not on a structurally sound line. It's inappropriate for an already-compromised or cracked pipe, which is why a camera inspection first matters when there's any doubt about the line's condition.

How do I know if it's root intrusion versus just grease buildup?

A camera inspection shows the difference clearly. Root intrusion looks like visible root growth inside the pipe; grease buildup looks like a coating narrowing the diameter. The symptoms from inside the house are often identical.

Should I try jetting myself with a rented machine?

It's generally not recommended without training. Hydro jetting equipment operates at high pressure and can cause real damage to an already-weak pipe if used incorrectly.
