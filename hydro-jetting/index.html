How It Works

Full-Diameter Cleaning, Not a Hole Through the Clog

A standard cable clears a path through a blockage by pushing or grinding through the middle of it. Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water nozzle that sprays outward as well as forward, scouring the entire interior wall of the pipe as it moves through. That's why a jetted line typically stays clear far longer than a cabled one on the same recurring clog.

When Jetting Isn't the Right Call

An Honest Limit

Jetting isn't appropriate for a pipe that's already compromised, an older or cracked line can be damaged further by high water pressure. We run a camera first if there's any doubt about the line's condition, and we'll recommend a repair instead of jetting when the pipe itself, not just what's inside it, is the actual problem.

Common Questions

Hydro Jetting FAQs

How is hydro jetting different from snaking a drain?

A cable pushes through the middle of a clog; jetting sprays high-pressure water outward and forward, scouring the full interior of the pipe. It clears more of the buildup that causes recurring clogs.

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

It depends on the pipe's condition. We check with a camera first if there's any doubt, since jetting can stress an already-compromised or cracked line. A sound line, even an older one, handles jetting fine.

How often should a line with root intrusion be jetted?

It depends on the severity and how quickly roots regrow, but a periodic schedule (often annually) is common for a property with a known history of root intrusion in its sewer line.

Is hydro jetting more expensive than snaking?

Generally yes, since it's a more thorough service, but it typically holds far longer on a recurring clog, which often makes it the better value over avoiding repeat cabling visits.
