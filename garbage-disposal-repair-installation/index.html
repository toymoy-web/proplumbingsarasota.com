Common Problems

What We're Usually Called About

🔊

Humming, Not Spinning

A humming sound with no grinding action almost always means the flywheel is jammed, usually clearable without replacing the unit.

💧

Leaking From Underneath

A leak at the sink flange, the dishwasher connection, or the discharge pipe are three different leak points that need different fixes, not automatically a sign the disposal itself has failed.

⚠

Reset Button Keeps Tripping

Frequent overheating and reset trips usually mean the motor is overworking against a partial jam or has reached the end of its service life.

Repair or Replace

When It's Worth Fixing

Worth Repairing

A jammed flywheel, a leak at a fitting or connection, or a tripped reset are all standard repairs on a unit that's otherwise working.

Worth Replacing

A motor that's burned out, a unit over 10 years old, or a leak coming from the housing itself (not a fitting) are all better solved with a new unit.

Local Consideration

Septic Systems and Garbage Disposals

A portion of Sarasota's older or more rural properties run on a septic system rather than municipal sewer, and a standard garbage disposal adds solid waste load a septic tank isn't necessarily sized for. For septic properties, we recommend and install septic-safe disposal units and can advise on usage habits that keep an existing septic system healthy.

Installation

What a New Disposal Install Involves

⚙

Correct Horsepower

We size the replacement disposal's horsepower to the household's actual use rather than defaulting to the cheapest available unit. An undersized motor jams more often under normal kitchen use.

🔌

Electrical Connection Check

Installation includes confirming the existing electrical connection and switch wiring are in good condition, not just bolting on a new unit to old wiring.

✅

Leak Test Before We Leave

Every new installation is run and checked for leaks at the flange, dishwasher connection, and discharge pipe before the job is considered done.

Extending Its Life

What Shortens a Disposal's Lifespan

Fibrous scraps like celery and corn husks, grease poured down the drain, and large volumes of ice all put unnecessary strain on a disposal's motor and blades over time. Running cold water during and briefly after use, rather than hot, also helps keep grease solid enough to grind rather than coating the chamber.

Common Questions

Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation FAQs

My disposal hums but doesn't spin. What's wrong?

The flywheel is almost certainly jammed. This is usually clearable without replacing the unit, and it's one of the most common disposal calls we get.

Is my leaking disposal repairable or do I need a new one?

It depends on where the leak is. A leak at the sink flange or a connection is repairable. A leak from the housing itself, meaning the unit's casing has actually cracked or corroded through, means the unit needs replacement.

Do I need a special garbage disposal if I'm on septic?

Yes, a septic-safe disposal is recommended for properties on a septic system, since a standard disposal adds more solid waste than some septic tanks are sized to handle comfortably, and using a regular disposal heavily can shorten the time between septic pumpings.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Most units last 8 to 12 years with normal use. A unit that's tripping its reset frequently or making a labored grinding sound at that age is a good candidate for replacement rather than another repair, since motor wear at that point tends to be progressive rather than a one-time fixable issue.
