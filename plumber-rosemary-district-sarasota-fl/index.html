The Neighborhood

From Overtown to Rosemary: The History Behind the Name

The area was part of the original plat of the Town of Sarasota in 1885, and Black residents began settling there in the 1890s. It was known as Overtown from the mid-1920s, and in 1994 it was officially named the Rosemary District, after Rosemary Cemetery, which was established in 1886 at Eighth Street and Central Avenue. Payne Chapel AME, at 513 Central Avenue, has stood in its current building since 1926.

Redevelopment brought a great deal of new housing. The City approved the Rosemary Residential Overlay District in 2014, and a June 2017 report counted more than 1,100 residential units in development, including the 229-apartment CitySide complex at 700 Cocoanut Avenue. Restaurants with outdoor seating, galleries and salvage shops now share the district with homes and apartments.

What It Means for Plumbing

Shared Buildings Change the Questions

In a condo or apartment building, the first plumbing question is often who owns the pipe. A leak that appears in one unit can start in a neighbor's unit or in a line the association maintains, and your governing documents usually spell out where the owner's responsibility ends. We trace the leak to its source and describe what we find, so the right party handles the repair.

Older single-family homes and small buildings in the district raise the same questions we ask everywhere. We look at the actual pipes rather than assuming from the age of the building, and we put what we find in plain terms.

Mixed-use blocks also bring commercial-style problems, such as busy kitchen drains and water heaters that serve more than one tenant. If you manage or own a business space here, call and describe the situation so we can tell you whether it is a fit.
