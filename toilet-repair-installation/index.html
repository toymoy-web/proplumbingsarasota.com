Common Calls

What We're Usually Fixing

💧

Running Toilet

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, an inexpensive repair that also stops wasting water and money on the utility bill.

⚠

Rocking at the Base

A toilet that rocks slightly usually means the wax ring seal has failed. It needs to be addressed before it causes a leak at the floor.

🚽

Frequent Clogging

A toilet that clogs often, even with normal use, can point to a partial blockage further down the line rather than a problem with the toilet itself.

Repair or Replace

When a New Toilet Makes Sense

Worth Repairing

Internal tank parts (flapper, fill valve, flush valve) and a failed wax ring are all standard, inexpensive repairs on an otherwise sound toilet.

Worth Replacing

A cracked bowl or tank, a toilet older than about 20 years using far more water per flush than current models, or a unit that's clogged repeatedly despite a clear line are all better solved with replacement.

Water Use

Older Toilets and Water Bills

A toilet installed before the mid-1990s can use two to three times the water per flush of a current model, and a running or slow-to-refill toilet compounds that waste further. For a household seeing a steadily high water bill without an obvious leak, an old high-flow toilet is worth ruling out before assuming the cause is a hidden pipe issue.

Installation Details

What We Check Beyond the Toilet Itself

Floor Flange Condition

A cracked or corroded floor flange under an old toilet is a common find during replacement and, if left unaddressed, will cause the same rocking and leaking problem with the new toilet. We inspect and repair the flange as part of any installation, not as an unexpected add-on charge afterward.

Bolt-Down and Level Check

A toilet that isn't seated level on an uneven floor will eventually stress the wax ring seal again. We shim and level as needed during installation so the new seal isn't fighting an uneven base from day one.

Installation

What a New Toilet Install Involves

Installation includes removing the old unit, replacing the wax ring seal (never reusing an old one), and confirming the shut-off valve at the wall is working correctly before the new toilet is set. We test for a solid seal at the base before considering the job finished.

Pricing

What Affects the Price of Toilet Repair & Installation

Every job is priced after we've seen it. The real factors: which specific part has failed, whether the floor flange needs repair, whether it's a repair or a full replacement, and the toilet's rough-in configuration.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden, accidental damage, like a burst pipe. They usually don't cover the underlying repair itself, or normal wear-related failures. It's worth checking your specific policy rather than assuming either way.

Common Questions

Toilet Repair & Installation FAQs

Why does my toilet run constantly?

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve. It's a quick, inexpensive repair and it stops wasting water and money on the utility bill in the meantime.

My toilet rocks slightly when I sit down. Is that serious?

It usually means the wax ring seal underneath has failed. It's worth fixing before it leaks at the floor, not after, since a failed seal left alone can eventually damage the subfloor underneath.

Should I repair my old toilet or replace it?

Internal tank parts and a wax ring are worth repairing on a sound toilet. A cracked bowl, a toilet older than about 20 years, or one that keeps clogging despite a clear line usually makes more sense to replace, especially once repair costs start approaching what a new unit would cost installed.

Can you install a toilet I've already purchased?

Yes, we install customer-supplied toilets as long as the rough-in measurement matches the existing plumbing. We'll also check the shut-off valve and supply line while the old unit is out.
