The Solution Gap

Why "I Think There's a Leak" Is a Hard Problem to Solve Yourself

A rising water bill with no visible water anywhere in the house is one of the most common reasons Sarasota homeowners call a plumber, and it's also one of the hardest to self-diagnose. The leak could be underground between the meter and the house, inside a slab, or behind a wall, and each location needs a different detection method. Guessing and opening drywall or concrete on a hunch is expensive and often wrong; a proper leak detection visit narrows the location before anything gets cut open.

Local Cause

Why Sarasota Homes See This Often

Salt Air and Older Copper

Homes built before CPVC and PEX became standard here often still have galvanized or copper supply lines, and Sarasota's tropical humidity and salt air speed up the corrosion that eventually causes a pinhole leak. These leaks are often small enough that no water is visible on the surface for weeks.

High Water Table and Slab Movement

The area's high water table and sandy soil mean slabs shift slightly over time. A common cause of the slab leaks that show up as a warm spot on the floor near a hot water line, or a small unexplained puddle near where a slab meets a garage.

How We Find It

Detection Methods We Actually Use

🎧

Acoustic Leak Detection

Ground and wall microphones pick up the specific sound of pressurized water escaping a pipe. Narrows the search to a small section before any digging or cutting.

🌡

Thermal Imaging

A thermal camera can reveal the temperature difference a hot-water leak creates behind drywall or under flooring without removing any material.

📊

Meter Isolation Test

Isolating sections of the plumbing system while watching the meter confirms whether the leak is on the supply side, the house side, or an irrigation line. Often the fastest way to rule out a slab leak entirely.

What Happens Next

From Detection to Repair

A Written Location, Not a Guess

Once a leak is located, we mark and explain exactly where it is before any repair pricing is discussed. For a supply-line leak reachable without major demolition, the fix is usually a straightforward section replacement. For a leak confirmed under a slab, we walk through the realistic options: a spot repair accessed through the slab, or rerouting the line above the slab entirely, along with the honest tradeoffs of each.

When It's Not a Leak at All

Not every high water bill traces back to a leak. Irrigation timers left running, a running toilet flapper, or a water softener stuck in regeneration can all cause the same symptom. Ruling these out first, before assuming a hidden pipe leak, is part of a proper detection visit and can save a homeowner from an unnecessary slab investigation.

Pricing

What Affects the Price of Leak Detection

Every job is priced after we've actually seen it. The real factors: which detection method the situation calls for (acoustic, thermal, or meter isolation), the suspected location (wall, slab, or yard line), how accessible that location is, and whether a repair or a full reroute follows.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden, accidental damage, like a burst pipe. They usually don't cover the underlying repair itself, or normal wear-related failures. It's worth checking your specific policy rather than assuming either way.

Common Questions

Leak Detection FAQs

My water bill went up but I don't see any water. Could it still be a leak?

Yes, this is one of the most common ways a leak shows up. An underground supply line or a slab leak can run for weeks without any visible water inside the house.

Do you have to cut into walls or the slab to find a leak?

Usually not to find it. Acoustic and thermal detection narrow the location first, so any cutting that follows is limited to the actual leak site rather than a guess.

How much does leak detection cost?

It depends on the suspected location (interior wall, slab, or yard line) and which detection method the situation calls for. We give you a price for the detection visit itself before starting.

What happens after you find the leak?

We explain exactly where it is and what the repair involves, including whether it's a spot repair or, for older galvanized systems with repeated leaks, whether repiping makes more sense long-term.
