By Community

Established Osprey vs. The Oaks Club

Older, Established Osprey

Closer to US-41 and the water, Osprey's older neighborhoods carry housing stock that predates most of the gated development further inland. Supply-line age and general wear are the more common calls in this part of the community.

The Oaks Club and Gated Development

The Oaks Club, one of the area's more prestigious private golf communities, anchors a cluster of newer, gated development inland from US-41. Irrigation-connected backflow testing and fixture-level work are the more common calls here, similar to what we see in other golf-anchored communities in our service area.

Local Landmark

Historic Spanish Point and What It Says About the Area

Historic Spanish Point, a waterfront museum and archaeological site on Little Sarasota Bay, is Osprey's best-known landmark and one of the oldest continuously documented sites on this part of the Gulf Coast. The same waterfront position that makes it historically significant also means nearby homes share the salt-air corrosion and high-water-table drainage concerns common to bayfront properties throughout our service area.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Osprey

🛑

Shut Off the Main Valve

If you can safely reach it, turn off your home's main water valve first. In Osprey's older sections near US-41, confirm where this is before you need it in a hurry.

⚡

Cut Power to Wet Areas

If water is anywhere near outlets or a breaker panel, shut off power to that specific area at the breaker, but only if you can reach it without stepping through standing water.

📞

Clear the Area and Call

Move anything valuable away from the water and call our 24/7 emergency line. We dispatch to every part of Osprey, established and gated alike, around the clock.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Osprey?

Do you offer 24/7 emergency plumbing in Osprey?

Does The Oaks Club need backflow testing?

Most properties there do. Like other golf-anchored communities in our service area, The Oaks Club's irrigation systems are typically tied to the potable water supply, which falls under the local utility's annual backflow testing requirement.
